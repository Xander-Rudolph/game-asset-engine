Characters in this pipeline start as a Qwen-Image painting. That painting becomes a TRELLIS or Hunyuan3D mesh, and UniRig rigs it with bones named `bone_0` to `bone_N` and no face at all ([rigging](/guide/rigging#bone-names-are-not-human-readable)). Daz 3D's Genesis figures are the opposite: a fixed, posable human with named bones, joint correctives and a face rig. <!-- DAZ-072, DAZ-079, DAZ-080, DAZ-083 --> Could Genesis be the human base for the sprite pipeline? Can it run on a Linux box with no desktop session? And what may a game sold worldwide ship?

Source Filmmaker has [its own note](/reference/source-filmmaker). Phonemes, visemes and facial rigs for generated meshes are covered in [lip sync](/reference/lip-sync).

## The answer in brief

- **Renders ship; 3D data needs an extra licence.** <!-- DAZ-001, DAZ-003 --> Under the standard Daz EULA you may sell 2D renders and animation frames, sprites included, unless a product page says otherwise. <!-- DAZ-001, DAZ-017, DAZ-018 --> Shipping the mesh, morphs or textures needs an Interactive License per product, where its creator offers one, or a separate signed agreement. <!-- DAZ-003, DAZ-017 --> Under the Interactive License the content must stay out of native formats and be protected against extraction ([EULA](https://www.daz3d.com/eula), [Interactive License page](https://www.daz3d.com/interactive-license-info), checked 2026-09-15). <!-- DAZ-004 -->
- **Daz content is a poor input to the AI stages.** The EULA excludes Content, "in whole or in part", from any AI system that can auto-generate material "derivative, imitative, or otherwise plagiaristic of the Content". <!-- DAZ-007 --> A Genesis mesh fed to a model is the plain case; whether a render counts is not said. <!-- DAZ-007, DAZ-008 -->
- **Genesis has a face rig.** <!-- DAZ-083 --> Genesis 8, 8.1 and 9 all carry the same 17 viseme dials, as pose controllers rather than blendshapes, and 8.1 and 9 add a FACS layer. <!-- DAZ-083, DAZ-084, DAZ-086 --> Rendered in clay from an imported Genesis 9 on 2026-09-16, a viseme changed 0 to 4 pixels of a 128 px whole figure, but 533 to 1,873 pixels of a 340 px head, much as MPFB2's did ([Genesis 9 visemes at sprite sizes](#genesis-9-visemes-at-sprite-sizes)).
- **Daz Studio has no Linux build.** <!-- DAZ-043 --> On Linux it runs only under unsupported Wine. <!-- DAZ-043 --> In the container the practical route is the Diffeomorphic importer in Blender, which has no explicit handling for background mode. <!-- DAZ-035, DAZ-036 --> It ran headless all the same: on 2026-09-16 `scripts/daz_import_probe.py` imported Genesis 9 with its anatomy figures and FACS controllers in the container's `bpy` 4.5.9 in under 6 s, with no change to the image ([Genesis 9 through the importer](#genesis-9-through-the-importer)).
- **The look does not match painted concept art**, and a diffusion restyle is the step the AI clause puts in doubt. <!-- DAZ-008 -->
- **MakeHuman through MPFB2 is the cleaner base.** Its bundled assets and face packs are CC0 1.0 (content) and its code is GPL-3.0-or-later ([MPFB2 licence](https://github.com/makehumancommunity/mpfb2/blob/master/LICENSE.md), checked 2026-09-15). <!-- DAZ-093, DAZ-095 --> Its visemes are shape keys. <!-- DAZ-095 --> On 2026-09-16 it ran headless in the container's `bpy` 4.5.9 through `scripts/mpfb_probe.py`, with no change to the image, and loaded all 15 Meta/Oculus-style visemes ([MakeHuman and MPFB2](#makehuman-and-mpfb2)).

## Anatomy of Genesis 8, 8.1 and 9

The statements in this section that carry a claim id were read from Daz's specification and file lists, from Diffeomorphic's data tables or from forum posts. The contents of a few `.dsf` files were read through a third-party GitHub copy of Daz content, which is not linked here and may differ from Daz's current files. The Genesis 8 and 8.1 statements were not measured here. The Genesis 9 statements were checked on 2026-09-16 against Genesis 9 Starter Essentials installed on this machine, with `scripts/daz_inventory.py` and `scripts/daz_library.py case-check` on the host and ad hoc `python3` reads of file names, first bytes, counts and formula outputs. Where a measurement bears on a statement, a measured passage follows it and names its script; none of them contradicted a Genesis 9 statement in this section.

### The files are JSON

Daz content is stored as DSON. <!-- DAZ-070 --> Specification 0.6.1.0 says DSON is JSON and its binary form "is merely a zlib compressed (aka 'zipped') version of the text format"; in practice Daz Studio writes gzip. <!-- DAZ-070 --> `.dsf` support files hold shared definitions, and `.duf` user files are top-level, user-facing files whose assets no other file can reference. <!-- DAZ-070 --> A morph is a set of sparse per-vertex deltas, and joint correctives follow bone rotations through formulas in the same files. <!-- DAZ-072 --> HD detail sits in a separate, undocumented binary `.dhdm`, so a base-resolution reader sees only base-mesh deltas. <!-- DAZ-074 -->

Measured on 2026-09-16 on Genesis 9 Starter Essentials:

- **The shipped files are not compressed.** `scripts/daz_inventory.py` read the 3,210 `.dsf` files as 3,209 plain JSON, 0 gzip and 1 not DSON. For the 637 `.duf` files, an ad hoc `python3` read of the first two bytes of every `.dsf` and `.duf` (2026-09-16, 17:45) found none starting with a gzip or zlib header, and `scripts/daz_library.py case-check` parsed all 3,847 as JSON with none unreadable. What Daz Studio writes when it saves a scene was not checked.
- **One `.dsf` is not DSON.** `Base/Tools/Geometry/Face Groups/Sculpting Optimized.dsf` is valid JSON whose only top-level key is `group_list`; `scripts/daz_inventory.py` skips it and says so.
- **Every HD morph also holds base deltas.** `scripts/daz_inventory.py` found 54 morphs with an `hd_url`, and all 54 carry base-resolution deltas too: 51 on the body (27 named `_HD2`, 24 `_HD3`), one `_HD3` on the Mouth and two on the Toon Hair. The `.dhdm` is on disk for 52; the Toon Hair's two are not in the product. No Genesis 9 file or modifier name contains `_div2`.

### Skeletons

Genesis 8 names are l/r-prefixed camelCase (`lShldrBend`, `lThighTwist`, `abdomenLower`), except ten toe segments with an underscore suffix (`lSmallToe1_2`) and eight face bones with a capital (`Nose`, `Chin`). <!-- DAZ-079 --> Of its bones, 77 sit under `head`, including `lowerJaw`, four tongue bones and 16 eyelid bones. <!-- DAZ-079 --> Those are DSON node names, and 27 of the 170 bones carry a node ID that differs from the name (`lThigh`, `chest_2`, and typos such as `lLipCorver`). <!-- DAZ-079 --> Every bone also has a spaced English label, so check which identifier an exporter writes before matching on names. <!-- DAZ-079 -->

Genesis 9 names are lowercase with an `l_` or `r_` prefix and compound words run together: `l_thigh`, `spine1` to `spine4`, `l_upperarm`, `l_forearmtwist1`, `neck1`. <!-- DAZ-080 --> Its face rig is simpler, at 42 bones, with five tongue bones. <!-- DAZ-080 -->

Measured on 2026-09-16 with `scripts/daz_inventory.py` and an ad hoc read of the bone parents: all 138 bone names in `Genesis9.dsf` are lowercase, 120 with `l_` or `r_` and 18 central ones with neither, and every name equals its id. 37 of them descend from `head`, and the five tongue bones arrive with the Mouth figure, which makes the 42. An imported armature holds more: `scripts/daz_import_probe.py` found the 143 Daz bones once the Mouth's rig was merged into the body's, plus 34 helper bones with `(drv)` in their names that the importer adds, 177 in all, 132 of them deform bones ([Genesis 9 through the importer](#genesis-9-through-the-importer)).

Either skeleton keeps stable, readable names, unlike UniRig's `bone_N` output, whose count changes per figure ([rigging](/guide/rigging#bone-names-are-not-human-readable)). <!-- DAZ-079, DAZ-080 --> Retargeting this repo's Mixamo or mesh2motion clips onto Genesis was not tried.

### Faces

All three generations carry the same 17 viseme names, AA, EE, EH, ER, F, IH, IY, K, L, M, OW, S, SH, T, TH, UW and W, under Pose Controls > Head > Visemes. <!-- DAZ-083 --> They are pose controllers, not ready-made blendshapes. <!-- DAZ-083 --> The Genesis 8 viseme files contain no morph deltas and only move face bones, and on 8.1 and 9 the visemes blend FACS shapes with jaw joints. <!-- DAZ-083 --> Getting them into an engine as blendshapes therefore means baking the bone-driven part. <!-- DAZ-083 -->

**Genesis 8.1** adds four FACS families: `facs_bs_` shape units, most of them HD `_div2` morphs with a `.dhdm`; `facs_cbs_` correctives; bone-driven `facs_jnt_` units; and `facs_ctrl_` controllers. <!-- DAZ-084 --> Its eight expression controllers, Afraid to Surprised, sit in a separate `FACSExpressions` folder. <!-- DAZ-084 -->

**Genesis 9** has no `facs_jnt_` family. <!-- DAZ-086 --> `facs_bs_JawOpen` is a vertex morph whose formulas also rotate `lowerjaw`, and eye look is bone-driven. <!-- DAZ-086 --> Its base FACS folder has no `.dhdm`; the HD variants sit in `FACS Details`. <!-- DAZ-086 --> The separate Mouth figure carries 10 viseme controllers of its own. <!-- DAZ-078 --> Its FACS names use Apple's ARKit vocabulary, but the set is not a one-to-one ARKit-52 set: Daz splits many shapes into left and right or upper and lower parts, with a controller on top. <!-- DAZ-087 -->

**Genesis 9, measured on 2026-09-16** with `scripts/daz_inventory.py` on the installed product and ad hoc `python3` reads of file names and formula outputs:

- **The visemes.** `facs_ctrl_vAA` to `facs_ctrl_vW` are 17 files in `Base/Morphs/Daz 3D/FACS`, grouped under `/Pose Controls/Head/Visemes`, with no morph block. Their 123 formula outputs all set other modifiers' values, `facs_bs_JawOpen` among them in 16 of the 17, and none moves a bone directly. `facs_bs_JawOpen` holds 3,164 deltas, and its own formulas rotate and translate `lowerjaw`. `facs_ctrl_EyeLookUp-DownLeft` and `facs_ctrl_EyeLookSide-SideLeft` rotate `l_eye`.
- **The families.** No file or modifier under Genesis 9 is `facs_jnt_`. The base `FACS` folder holds no `.dhdm`; `FACS Details` holds 27, with 27 `_HD2` `.dsf`. The Mouth figure carries the 10 viseme controllers `facs_ctrl_vER`, `vIH`, `vIY`, `vL`, `vOW`, `vS`, `vSH`, `vT`, `vTH` and `vUW`, and the Toon Mouth the same 10.
- **Alias files.** The base `FACS` folder holds 535 `.dsf` and 220 `.png`: 161 `facs_bs_`, 25 `facs_cbs_`, 99 `facs_ctrl_` and 250 `alias_` files. An alias file gives another modifier's channel a second name, with channel type `alias`, no deltas and no formulas. The body has 398 of them across six morph folders, 17 of them carrying viseme names, so a count that takes aliases for morphs finds 34 visemes. Leaving aliases and 5 other modifiers aside, the body has 1,114 morphs: 17 `facs_ctrl_v`, 204 `facs_bs_`, 25 `facs_cbs_`, 103 `body_cbs_`, no `eCTRLv`, `facs_jnt_` or `pJCM`, and 765 others.
- **The splits.** `facs_bs_CheekPuffLeft` and `Right` with `facs_ctrl_CheekPuff`, and four `facs_bs_MouthClose` parts with `facs_ctrl_MouthClose`, all exist. That each controller drives its parts was not checked.

The free Genesis 9 base includes the viseme controllers, but the 40-dial Genesis 9 Expressions set is a separate [US$21.98 product](https://www.daz3d.com/genesis-9-expressions) (checked 2026-09-15). <!-- DAZ-052 --> The free product's page never mentions visemes or FACS, so read its file list. <!-- DAZ-049 --> For emotions on a dialogue portrait with no purchase, Genesis 8.1 is a free option: its eight `FACSExpressions` controllers ship in Genesis 8 Starter Essentials. <!-- DAZ-051, DAZ-084 -->

[Lip sync](/reference/lip-sync#the-mapping-to-adopt) maps Rhubarb's nine letters onto the 17 Genesis dials; that column has not been tried on a figure. The third-party [Emphasized Visemes for Genesis 9](https://www.daz3d.com/emphasized-visemes-for-genesis-9) by dobit (US$19.98, SKU 88578, checked 2026-09-15) has 15 dials: AA, CH, DD, E, FF, I, K, O, PP, RR, S, TH, U, W and SIL. <!-- DAZ-053 --> Those are close to the Oculus fifteen in [lip sync's table](/reference/lip-sync#mouth-shape-sets), with W where Oculus has nn. Diffeomorphic is reported to map Moho `.dat` lip sync onto Genesis visemes, with a Rhubarb table among others (read from its wiki, not verified). <!-- DAZ-034 --> Its tables ship in a GPL-2.0-or-later add-on, so copy none of them into this repository. <!-- DAZ-026 -->

## Getting Genesis into Blender on Linux

### Daz Studio runs on Windows and macOS

Daz Studio is free, proprietary software for Windows and macOS only; Linux users can run it only under unsupported Wine. <!-- DAZ-043 --> The current general release is 6.25 (build 6.25.2026.14722, 2026-06-04), and the final Daz Studio 4 general release, 4.24.0.4, installs alongside it. <!-- DAZ-043, DAZ-044 --> The official bridges and Diffeomorphic's `.dbz` export script run inside Daz Studio, so they are Windows and macOS only too. <!-- DAZ-033, DAZ-043 -->

Forum reports from 2025 have Daz Studio 4.x running under Lutris-managed Wine, with DIM installing content in the same prefix, and Iray GPU rendering working for some users on nvidia-libs with wine-staging 10.9 or 10.14. <!-- DAZ-045 --> Others on the same thread could not get the GPU detected, or lost it after kernel or driver updates. <!-- DAZ-045 --> Daz Studio 6 did not run under Lutris and Wine as of 2025-06. <!-- DAZ-045 --> Whether any of these setups runs without a display was not checked.

The Daz to Blender Bridge is a Daz Studio plugin that writes an FBX plus a `.dtu` JSON file. <!-- DAZ-025 --> Plain FBX export keeps no ERC links, so joint correctives do not fire when the figure is posed (forum reports). <!-- DAZ-089 -->

### Installing the content by hand

No Daz software is needed to install a product. Measured on 2026-09-16 on the host's `python3` 3.13, with Genesis 9 Starter Essentials (SKU 86958) downloaded from the owner's Daz account in a browser as three DIM packages, `IM00086958-01_Genesis9StarterEssentials1Of3.zip` to `IM00086958-03_Genesis9StarterEssentials3Of3.zip`. The steps are in the [Daz figures](/guide/daz-figures) guide.

- **The packages.** Each holds a `Manifest.dsx` whose `File` elements, 4,528, 197 and 1,780 of them, are all `TARGET="Content"` and `ACTION="Install"`, and no zip entry is left unlisted. The three parts merge into one content directory, and three `Runtime/Support/DAZ_3D_86958_*` files are in all three with identical bytes.
- **Installing.** [`scripts/daz_library.py`](https://github.com/Xander-Rudolph/game-asset-engine/blob/main/scripts/daz_library.py) `install` extracts only those entries into `MODELS_DIR/daz_library`, refuses a library inside the repository or one that contains it, and lets a file land only after its CRC-32 check passes. All three parts gave 6,499 files and 2,452,887,819 bytes, in 7.59 s and then 6.90 s wall into an empty library, to the page cache with no fsync; those times came from the script before the review fixes of the same day. With the current script, `verify 86958 --crc` passed on 6,499 of 6,499 files in 0.51 to 0.93 s, and `selftest` passed 67 checks.
- **The record.** `.daz_library/86958.json` lists every file, each zip's sha256 and the licence held: the Standard License unless `--interactive-license` records a bought Interactive License, which the script cannot check. The date the EULA was last read stays empty until it is given with `scripts/daz_library.py licence 86958 --eula-read YYYY-MM-DD`, and on this machine it is still empty.
- **Case.** No folder in the library holds two names that differ only in case. But `scripts/daz_library.py case-check` read 24,036 references to 5,185 distinct paths in the product's 3,847 `.dsf` and `.duf` files in 7.22 s, and 76 of those paths match a file only when case is ignored: 52 write `runtime/` for `Runtime/`, 18 `DAZ 3D` for `Daz 3D`, 4 `Daz` for `DAZ`, and 2 differ in a file name. Another 93 point at files the product does not ship, such as Daz Studio's built-in FilaToon and PBRSkin shaders. A reader on Linux has to match case the way Windows does.
- **How the importer copes.** On Linux Diffeomorphic's "Case-Sensitive Paths" setting defaults to on, and then it lowercases each reference and matches every folder and file name without regard to case (read in `settings.py` at the `version_5_2_0` tag). That is how its FACS table, which names `data/DAZ 3D/Genesis 9/...`, found the library's `Daz 3D` folder. The 76 were not tried one by one: following `.duf` and `.dsf` references from each build's figure and anatomy files, in an ad hoc read on 2026-09-16, reaches none of them from `Genesis 9.duf` and one, a thumbnail `.png`, from the Kat character preset. The Kat import's log named only `PBRSkin.dsf` as not found.

### Diffeomorphic reads the content files directly

The Diffeomorphic DAZ Importer (`import_daz`) is GPL-2.0-or-later for its code only, and it imports native `.duf` and `.dsf` files. <!-- DAZ-026 --> Version 5.2.0 (2026-08-10) declares `blender_version_min` 4.2.0 and was tested on Blender 3.6 and 5.2. <!-- DAZ-028 --> The container's `bpy` 4.5.9 is inside that range. <!-- DAZ-028 --> The importer ran on it on 2026-09-16 ([Genesis 9 through the importer](#genesis-9-through-the-importer)). Master's `bl_info` already carries the unreleased 5.3.0, so install from the `version_5_2_0` tag. <!-- DAZ-028 -->

The rest of this subsection was read from the importer's source at master commit `3437e54`, its wiki and its blog. Where running the `version_5_2_0` tag on Genesis 9 bore on a statement, a measured sentence follows it.

**It needs no Daz Studio to read files.** <!-- DAZ-033, DAZ-035 --> It opens `.duf` and `.dsf` itself, gzip first and plain text second. <!-- DAZ-035 --> Content roots are a plain list that a script sets with `import_daz.set_global_setting('contentDirs', [...])`. <!-- DAZ-035 --> The bulk imports behind `useVisemes` and `useFacs` read per-figure tables that hard-code Daz's own `data/DAZ 3D/...` folders, so the content must keep Daz's layout. <!-- DAZ-037 --> The installed library spells that folder `Daz 3D`, and the tables still found it, because on Linux the importer matches names without regard to case ([installing the content by hand](#installing-the-content-by-hand)). DIM packages are reported as `<prefix><SKU>-<part>_Name.zip` files with a `Manifest.dsx` and a `Content` folder, whose `data`, `People` and `Runtime` subfolders merge into one content directory (Daz's work-in-progress docs and a forum thread, not verified). <!-- DAZ-091 --> The three Genesis 9 Starter Essentials packages have that layout, as measured above.

::: warning A wrong content path fails silently
A content directory that does not exist raises no `DazError`. <!-- DAZ-035 --> The importer drops it, and an unresolvable reference makes `get_absolute_paths()` return an empty list with no message at the default verbosity. <!-- DAZ-035 --> Run on 2026-09-16 with `contentDirs` set to a folder that does not exist, `set_global_setting` raised nothing, `get_root_paths()` and `get_absolute_paths()` returned empty lists, and `easy_import_daz` returned `FINISHED` having built no object. At verbosity 3, `get_error_message()` did then say `Some assets were not found. Check that all DAZ root paths have been set up correctly.` and list the missing files; the default verbosity 2 was not tried. Check that the directories exist and that the figure's path resolves before importing anything.
:::

**It is scriptable.** <!-- DAZ-031, DAZ-032 --> From 4.4.0 (2025-04-06) the calls are `bpy.ops.daz.easy_import_daz(directory=..., files=[{"name": "x.duf"}])` and `bpy.ops.daz.import_visemes()`, after which `rig["eCTRLvAA"] = 1.0` sets a viseme. <!-- DAZ-031 --> On Genesis 9 that last step does not apply: run on 2026-09-16, `import_visemes()` loaded no morph and made no `eCTRLvAA`, and the visemes came from `bpy.ops.daz.import_facs()` as `facs_ctrl_vAA` to `facs_ctrl_vW`. `set_silent_mode(True)` sends errors to the terminal instead of a pop-up, `import_daz.get_error_message()` returns them, and an extension installed into Blender's default repository imports as `bl_ext.user_default.import_daz`. <!-- DAZ-032 -->

**Headless use is unhandled.** <!-- DAZ-036 --> No file references `bpy.app.background`, and all nine `invoke_props_dialog` calls sit on operator `invoke()` paths. <!-- DAZ-036 --> Neither the code nor the wiki mentions `blender -b` or `--background`. <!-- DAZ-036 --> Calling the operators from Python avoids those dialogs: on 2026-09-16 `easy_import_daz`, `merge_rigs`, `import_visemes` and `import_facs` all ran in background mode, and none waited on one.

**Morphs arrive as drivers, approximately.** <!-- DAZ-030 --> Morphs import as rig property sliders whose drivers move shape keys, bones or both. <!-- DAZ-030 --> TCB splines are approximated with `smoothstep()`, and morphs that change the rest pose are ignored by default. <!-- DAZ-030 -->

**The best path wants a file from Daz Studio.** <!-- DAZ-033 --> Default fitting reads a `.dbz` written by a script inside Daz Studio. <!-- DAZ-033 --> Since 2021-03-24 a `.duf` can be imported without one, using Mesh Fitting "Morphed (Characters)", but the wiki warns "Not all shapekeys are found" and "Shapekeys are not transferred to clothes". <!-- DAZ-033 --> Morphed fitting imported `Genesis 9.duf` with no `.dbz` on 2026-09-16; what it missed against a `.dbz` import was not checked.

### Rendering

Diffeomorphic converts Daz's Iray materials three ways, and its own labels admit the loss: "BSDF (Cycles Only)" is "For Cycles, best IRAY quality", "Extended Principled" is "For Eevee and Cycles, limited IRAY quality", and "FBX Compatible" has "very limited IRAY quality". <!-- DAZ-039 --> FilaToon shaders get a dedicated toon node tree. <!-- DAZ-039 --> `scripts/render_sheet.py` renders with `BLENDER_EEVEE_NEXT`, a transparent film, a sun light that rides the camera and, by default, an orthographic camera (read from the script), so "limited IRAY quality" is the best on offer. <!-- DAZ-039 --> Measured on 2026-09-16 while building `scripts/mpfb_probe.py`, EEVEE in the container renders through llvmpipe, Mesa's software OpenGL, not on the graphics card: about 6 s a frame at 64 samples and about 1.7 s at 16.

Measured on Genesis 9 on 2026-09-16 with `scripts/daz_import_probe.py render`, EEVEE again reporting `llvmpipe (LLVM 15.0.7, 256 bits)`, in clay unless `--materials` is named. Peak memory is the Blender process's peak RSS, and the container's range comes from `docker stats`.

- **Subdivision is the cost.** The importer saves 5 Subsurf modifiers at level 1 in the viewport and up to 3 for render. At those levels, `render --blend output/daz/g9_visemes.blend --no-sheet --subdivision as-saved --only AA --sizes 128 --columns face --samples 16` drew 2 face cells of 128 px in 88.7 s, peak 4,593 MiB, the container rising from 4.54 to 8.83 GiB. Without `--no-sheet --subdivision as-saved`, so with Subsurf off, the default, and with `render_sheet.py`'s 2 rows first in 28.8 s wall, the probe drew the same 2 cells in 17.9 s, peak 2,655 MiB. A `render_sheet.py` whole-figure cell at the importer's levels and 64 samples took about 238 s, so 18 rows would pass its 3,600 s timeout; that run, by an earlier version of the probe that handed `render_sheet.py` the file as saved, was stopped after 3 cells. The probe now gives `render_sheet.py` a temporary copy with Subsurf off.
- **A full run with Subsurf off.** `render --blend output/daz/g9_cage.blend`, run once with an earlier version of the probe that renders the same rows for this file, and not re-run: `render_sheet.py`'s 18 whole-figure rows at 128 px in 184.3 s wall, then the probe's 162 cells, the neutral row and 17 visemes in 3 framings at 128, 220 and 340 px and 64 samples, in 1,287.6 s wall, about 8 s a cell. Peak 3,401 MiB, the container rising from 4.54 to 7.67 GiB. What those cells show is in [Genesis 9 visemes at sprite sizes](#genesis-9-visemes-at-sprite-sizes).
- **Textures.** The Kat character preset, `People/Genesis 9/Characters/Kat for Genesis 9.duf`, references 26 texture maps, 4 of them 8,192 px square and 16 of 4,096 (a header read on the host). Building it with "Extended Principled" took 8.7 s wall, and the figure import alone peaked at 1,444 MiB, because the importer reads the images as it imports; `--no-textures` clears them only afterwards and so did not lower that peak, at 1,456 MiB. At render time it matters: `render --blend output/daz/g9_kat.blend --no-sheet --materials --sizes 340 --columns face --only AA --samples 16` drew 2 cells in 81.9 s, peak 6,312 MiB, the container rising from 4.54 to 10.63 GiB, against 27.7 s and 2,836 MiB for the build with its images cleared. The importer's own resize operator, `daz.set_resolution`, was not tried.
- **Where frames go.** Everything the probe writes from Daz content is in the gitignored `output/daz/`, except `render_sheet.py`'s temporary cells, which it writes to `output/_sheet_frames/` and deletes when it finishes; the probe deletes that folder too when it stops a `render_sheet.py` job.

## How it would fit this sprite pipeline

**Where it slots in.** For a human, Genesis would replace concept art, mesh generation and rigging in one step, giving a Blender scene with named bones and a face. <!-- DAZ-030, DAZ-031 --> When this was researched, `render_sheet.py` opened only `.fbx`, `.glb`, `.gltf` or `.obj` and posed bones only (read from the script), so each pose or viseme would have needed its own baked export. Since 2026-09-16 it also opens a `.blend` as saved, with its drivers, and sets shape keys and rig properties per pose; it has rendered MPFB2's visemes that way, and on the same day the 17 visemes of an imported Genesis 9 through `"@props"` ([rendering](#rendering)). Genesis 8's visemes, like its face units, only move face bones, so they are face data its `--poses transforms:` path could carry; that is untested. <!-- DAZ-030, DAZ-083 --> Under the standard Daz EULA the sheet may ship, but the `.blend` and any exported FBX may not. <!-- DAZ-001, DAZ-002 -->

**It should not feed the AI stages.** A Daz render used as a Qwen-Image reference, a ControlNet input or a TRELLIS image is the use the AI exclusion puts in doubt (see [the AI clauses](#the-ai-clauses)). <!-- DAZ-008 --> The doubt covers renders only. <!-- DAZ-008 --> Loading the Genesis mesh, its textures or its UV maps into Hunyuan3D 2.1 texturing, UniRig or any other model is the plain case of the exclusion: Content input to a system that auto-generates material from it. <!-- DAZ-007 --> Texturing is the tempting case, because that stage paints a mesh to match a concept ([textures](/guide/textures)), and Hunyuan3D's territory exclusion would stack on top ([licensing](/guide/licensing#hunyuan3d-2-and-2-1)).

**The face pays off at portrait size.** Sprites here are 128 px for a map token and 220 px for a close-up, and portraits start at 340 px ([facings](/guide/facings#sizes)). A viseme mouth can read in a dialogue portrait; at 128 px the rig's value is consistent proportions and poses across a cast.

**The style mismatch.** The house concept prompt asks for a "Detailed painted fantasy illustration with realistic materials and lighting", and its negative prompt excludes "cartoon, cel shaded, anime, flat vector art" ([concept art](/guide/concept-art#what-a-good-prompt-for-this-pipeline-says)). Genesis ships Iray materials: the Genesis 9 Starter Essentials product page alone lists 753 maps of 256 by 256 to 8192 by 8192. <!-- DAZ-039, DAZ-049 --> That count was not reproduced: a header read of the installed `Runtime/Textures` on 2026-09-16 found 762 image files (648 `.jpg`, 113 `.png`, 1 `.tif`), 8 of them 8,192 px on their largest side, 395 of 4,096 and 45 under 256. Which files Daz counts as maps was not checked. The gap is texture detail and skin realism against painted realistic materials, not the shading model. Three ways to narrow it:

- **Render under the same lights as everything else.** Import with "Extended Principled", the EEVEE material option, and render with `render_sheet.py`'s own `--key` and `--ambient` lighting, as the TRELLIS assets are, with the maps scaled down towards sprite size. <!-- DAZ-039 --> The "Extended Principled" import ran on 2026-09-16, on the Kat preset, and rendered in the probe's EEVEE framings ([rendering](#rendering)). Rendering it under `--key` and `--ambient` and scaling the maps down were not tried, and whether those materials survive a glTF export was not checked, so this may need the `.blend` input `render_sheet.py` now has.
- **Restyle through diffusion.** Likely the closest match to the painted art, but the option the AI clause puts most in doubt. <!-- DAZ-008 -->
- **Toon content, for a cel-style project only.** The free Genesis 9 Starter Essentials includes Base Anime Feminine and Masculine, Toon Base clothing variants and Toon Base Anime Hair. <!-- DAZ-049 --> It also ships the six Toon sub-figures in the table above and a `Base Anime FACS` folder of 12 `_NoJaw` variants. <!-- DAZ-078, DAZ-086 --> Blender's Shader to RGB node, the usual base of a toon ramp, works only on EEVEE and breaks the PBR pipeline and every render pass except Combined. <!-- DAZ-041 --> This pipeline's own concepts exclude that look.

### Genesis as a shape donor

[Lip sync](/reference/lip-sync#heads-to-borrow-shapes-from) lists heads whose shapes could be moved onto a generated mesh, and [measured a Surface Deform transfer](/reference/lip-sync#what-blender-does-headlessly) on test spheres. A Genesis head is the licence-heavy case. <!-- DAZ-012 -->

On a plain reading, Genesis shape keys transferred onto a TRELLIS or Hunyuan3D mesh are a derived three-dimensional work, which the EULA allows only if it is "designed to require or encourage the use of Content available through the online DAZ store" ([derived works](#derived-works)). <!-- DAZ-012 --> A generated mesh does neither, so the EULA's grant for derived 3D works does not cover it, and Daz may also demand at its sole discretion that distribution stop. <!-- DAZ-012 --> An Interactive License does not lift the condition. <!-- DAZ-012 --> Addendum 3.0 restates it for game use, where Daz may stop a work that "fails to require the use of Content", with no "or encourage". <!-- DAZ-012 --> Sprites rendered from such a mesh would otherwise fall under the 2D works clause, but the derived-works condition attaches to creating the work, not only to distributing it. <!-- DAZ-001, DAZ-012 --> All of this is a reading of the EULA text, not a ruling from Daz.

### MakeHuman and MPFB2

[MakeHuman's licence](https://github.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman/blob/master/LICENSE.md) puts its base mesh, targets, textures, clothes, poses and expressions under CC0 1.0 and claims nothing over output; assets from community repositories carry their own licences. <!-- DAZ-092 --> MPFB2, the Blender add-on, keeps that split, and its FAQ answers yes to selling models and to using them in a closed-source game. <!-- DAZ-093 --> Several official packs on MakeHuman's [asset index](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/assetpacks.html), such as Hair 02 and 03, Shirts 02 and 03 and Bodyparts 02 and 03, are CC BY rather than CC0, so dressing a character from them adds a credit. MPFB2 needs Blender 4.2 or newer, and v2.0.17 shipped on 2026-07-22. <!-- DAZ-094 -->

The face is what makes it a Genesis alternative. From v2.0.15, FaceService loads face packs as shape keys at value 0.0: 22 Microsoft-style visemes, 15 Meta/Oculus-style visemes (`viseme_aa`, `viseme_CH`, `viseme_sil`) and 52 ARKit face units. <!-- DAZ-095 --> The three packs are separate CC0 "functional asset packs" on the asset index, and FaceService loads from installed packs, so install them first. <!-- DAZ-095 -->

**It runs without a UI.** Measured on 2026-09-16 with [`scripts/mpfb_probe.py`](https://github.com/Xander-Rudolph/game-asset-engine/blob/main/scripts/mpfb_probe.py), which downloads MPFB 2.0.17 and the three face packs, pinned by size and sha256, into the gitignored `input/_devtools/mpfb2/`, then builds and renders a body in the container with no change to the image:

- **Enabling it.** MPFB ran in the container's `bpy` 4.5.9 once added as an extension from a local repository: `bpy.context.preferences.extensions.repos.new(...)` with `source='USER'`, then `addon_utils.extensions_refresh(ensure_wheels=False)` and `addon_utils.enable('bl_ext.<module>.mpfb', default_set=True)`. Put on `sys.path` instead, its `register()` fails with `ValueError: The "package" does not name an extension`. Setting `BLENDER_USER_RESOURCES` before `import bpy` kept its logs, config and installed packs out of Blender's own user folder.
- **Ending the process.** Once MPFB has been enabled, a `bpy` process does not exit at the end of its script, even after `addon_utils.disable()`: it prints `Error: Not freed memory blocks` and stays alive. The probe ends with `os._exit(0)`.
- **The visemes.** `bpy.ops.mpfb.load_pack` installed the packs from their zips, and `FaceService.load_targets(basemesh, load_microsoft_visemes=False, load_meta_visemes=True)` loaded the 15 Meta/Oculus visemes as shape keys at value 0. `viseme_sil` moves no vertex, and `viseme_aa` moves one by up to 0.01816 m. With no pack installed, `load_targets` raises nothing: it logs `Did not find matching target for viseme_aa` and adds no key.
- **Helper geometry.** `create_human()` leaves 5,778 helper vertices in its 19,158-vertex mesh, hidden only by a Mask modifier: 1,000 in joint cubes, and 4,778 for tights, skirt, hair, tongue, genitals, eyelashes, eyes and teeth. Exported with the mask in place, the helpers go into the glTF. `export_apply=True` drops every morph target, and applying the modifier fails with `RuntimeError: Error: Modifier cannot be applied to a mesh with shape keys`, so the probe deletes the helpers in Edit Mode. By default it keeps the 506 eye, teeth and tongue vertices, leaving 13,886. Eight visemes move the tongue and lower teeth helpers, so deleting every helper, as MPFB's own export copy does, loses that motion.
- **What comes out.** A `.blend`, which opened and rendered with no MPFB add-on enabled, and a `.glb` that keeps all 15 morph targets and re-imports into a clean `bpy` with Basis and 15 keys. A build took 1.6 to 1.8 s.
- **Rigs.** The built-in `game_engine` rig has 53 bones, with `neck_01` and `head` but no jaw, eye or tongue bone. `default` has 163, including `jaw`, `eye.L`, `eye.R` and eleven tongue bones. The probe adds a rig before deleting the helpers, in the order MPFB's Export Copy uses.

What reads at which size is in [lip sync](/reference/lip-sync#faces-only-read-on-a-portrait): on a whole figure the visemes barely show, and in a head framing at 340 px seven of the fourteen that move the mouth can be told apart. Still untested: the Microsoft-style and ARKit packs, which were installed but not loaded; MakeHuman's system assets pack, so the body had no skin material and only crude helper eyes and teeth; posing any rig; MPFB's Export Copy operator itself; and `FaceService.configure_lip_sync` with the Lip Sync add-on.

## What not to do

**Never put Daz 3D data in this repository or the container image. <!-- DAZ-002 --> Never put it in a game build without an Interactive License for each product used or a separate agreement signed by both parties, and under the Interactive License keep it out of native formats and protect it against extraction.** <!-- DAZ-002, DAZ-003, DAZ-004 --> The standard Daz EULA forbids distributing extractable Content and transferring Content without consent. <!-- DAZ-002 --> A published image containing the content library would be distribution ([redistributing](/guide/redistributing)). <!-- DAZ-002 --> `cleanup.py keep` copies what it is given into `output/assets/<name>/`, the curated asset folder ([cleanup](/guide/cleanup)). Since 2026-09-16 it refuses to copy Daz 3D data, judged by path and suffix, as item 6 in [What to build](#what-to-build) describes, but a mesh or texture copied out of `output/daz/` under another suffix passes, and its `sources.json` records only original paths, so nothing there would mark such a file as Daz data.

**Do not feed Daz renders to Qwen-Image, ControlNet, TRELLIS, Hunyuan3D or any training** for a game you sell until Daz answers in writing, **and never load the Genesis mesh, textures or UV maps into Hunyuan3D texturing, UniRig or any other model.** <!-- DAZ-007, DAZ-008, DAZ-009 -->

**Do not ship a generated mesh carrying Genesis-derived shape keys.** It is a derived 3D work that requires no Daz store content, which the derived-works grant does not cover, with or without an Interactive License. <!-- DAZ-012 --> It also ships Daz morph data. <!-- DAZ-002 -->

**Do not buy an Interactive License for a sprite-only game.** A stack of renders needs only the standard Daz EULA unless the product page says otherwise, and unless stated otherwise the add-on is not refundable. <!-- DAZ-017, DAZ-018 --> If 3D data might ship later, a Daz forum moderator said in 2022-09 that in-house development needs no Interactive License until "you have something ready to share"; that is a [forum post](https://www.daz3d.com/forums/discussion/588916), not the EULA. <!-- DAZ-019 -->

**Do not sell Daz-derived sprites as a separate in-game purchase without asking.** Under the Interactive License that needs written consent, and no ruling covers standard-licence renders. <!-- DAZ-005 -->

**Do not upload pipeline renders to Daz's galleries or forums.** An upload licenses Daz to modify them. <!-- DAZ-015 -->

**Do not script the Daz website.** <!-- DAZ-014 --> Download through Daz's installers or a browser.

**Do not plan on the bridges on Linux.** They run only inside Daz Studio, which has no Linux build. <!-- DAZ-025, DAZ-043 -->

**Do not ship MB-Lab meshes in a closed-source game.** Its data is AGPL-3.0-or-later, and generated models "must be distributed under AGPL 3". <!-- DAZ-098 --> **Do not ship SMPL or SMPL-X meshes in a commercial game without a commercial licence.** The CC BY 4.0 SMPL-Body subset is the exception, without shape blendshapes and with credit. <!-- DAZ-115, DAZ-116 -->

## Honest uncertainty

- **Whether a render is "Content" under the AI clause.** The licence is silent. <!-- DAZ-008 --> The only guidance is two moderator forum posts, and the 2026 post does not say Daz gave the answer. <!-- DAZ-009 -->
- **Selling standard-licence sprites as DLC.** Section 1.0 allows selling 2D works, and Addendum 3.0's sale ban is written for Interactive-licensed content. <!-- DAZ-001, DAZ-005 --> No ruling covers renders.
- **Sprites from a mesh carrying Genesis-derived shapes.** The derived-works condition attaches to creating the work, and no ruling covers renders of it. <!-- DAZ-012 -->
- **Character Creator content inside a game.** The Content EULA's Standard License allows commercial games and export to outside engines, but its 2.1 Restrictions (C) bars use "As embedded content within applications or online services", and the policy page's FAQ still carries older wording. <!-- DAZ-105 --> Which term governs a character shipped in a build is not settled; read the [Content EULA](https://www.reallusion.com/Content/EULA/EULA.htm) (checked 2026-09-15).
- **What an import without a `.dbz` loses on Genesis 9**, whose visemes are split across the body and Mouth figures, and whether its Toon sub-figures import at all. <!-- DAZ-033, DAZ-078 --> A Morphed import on 2026-09-16 brought both figures' viseme controllers, merged into one set of 17 properties, and logged `Missing geonode` for two `_HD3` morphs; no `.dbz` import was there to compare with, and no Toon sub-figure was imported.
- **Whether Genesis 8.1's `_div2` `.dsf` files carry useful base-resolution deltas**, or mostly point at `.dhdm` data. <!-- DAZ-074, DAZ-084 --> Two data points since, both from `scripts/daz_inventory.py` on 2026-09-16: 272 base deltas as well as an `hd_url` in a third-party copy of Genesis 8 Female 8.1's `facs_bs_BrowDownLeft_div2`, and base deltas in all 54 `hd_url` morphs of Genesis 9 Starter Essentials, which has no `_div2` file. Genesis 8 Starter Essentials is not installed, and whether any of those deltas are useful was not judged.
- **Whether a chat model may look at a Daz render.** The AI clause's examples include chatGPT. <!-- DAZ-007 --> The EULA does not say whether a render counts as Content "in part". <!-- DAZ-008 --> The by-eye reading in [Genesis 9 visemes at sprite sizes](#genesis-9-visemes-at-sprite-sizes) was made by Claude, also a chat model, the assistant that ran the probe, from a crop of its own 340 px render. Until the owner decides whether that is allowed, the `daz-figure` skill reads no Daz render into the conversation: the person opens the sheet and says what they see, and Claude gives the pixel counts.
- **How the Genesis 9 mouths read with skin.** Every judgement above is of clay renders. Kat's AA was rendered at 340 px with its maps, but not judged, and the importer's material conversion admits "limited IRAY quality" in EEVEE. <!-- DAZ-039 -->
- **The other case-only references.** On Linux the importer matches paths without regard to case by default, but only one of the 76 case-only references in Genesis 9 Starter Essentials, a thumbnail, is reachable from the figure and anatomy files the builds here imported. What any other Linux reader, or a material preset that writes `runtime/textures/...`, does with them was not tried.
- **Daz Studio without a display.** Daz documents `-headless` and `-noPrompt` flags and script rendering to file through `DzRenderMgr.doRender` (read, not verified). <!-- DAZ-064, DAZ-065 --> Whether they work under Wine with no display was not tried.
- **Daz Studio 6 under Wine.** A [2026-06 forum report](https://www.daz3d.com/forums/discussion/comment/9567361/) says it runs under Bottles, but it was not verified. <!-- DAZ-046 -->
- **MPFB2's other face packs and a skin material.** The Microsoft-style and ARKit packs were installed but not loaded, and the Meta/Oculus visemes, which did load without a UI on 2026-09-16, were rendered with no skin material ([MakeHuman and MPFB2](#makehuman-and-mpfb2)).
- **Unsettled licence readings.** Whether the Daz to Roblox Studio Exporter counts as an Apache-2.0 tool, and whether MetaHuman characters may ship outside Unreal, are set out above. <!-- DAZ-022, DAZ-110 -->
